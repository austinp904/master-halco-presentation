## What's in this product line

The Shoreline seawall offering is composed of two complementary products that work together:

1. **EcoWall** — High-rigidity fiber-reinforced sheet panels that form the wall face. The pitch deck describes them as **"high-rigidity fiber-reinforced panels"** that are *~70% stiffer than traditional vinyl sheet piling* at the same 0.25" wall profile and the same market price as standard vinyl sheets. The structural advantage is the killer differentiator.
2. **KingPile / EcoPile structural members** — heavier-duty piles used as the vertical structural members of a seawall (kingposts, batter pilings, or end-anchors). The `eco_kingpile (2).jpg` image in this folder shows the KingPile profile. KingPile is a heavier-duty variant of EcoPile engineered for the higher loading typical of seawall service.

Together, they form a **complete recycled-composite seawall system** — the panels handle lateral earth-and-water pressure, the king-piles and batter-piles handle the structural loading and tieback connections.

## EcoPile / KingPile in seawall service today

Even before EcoWall is fully productized for the Master Halco channel, **EcoPile is already being used in seawall installations** as batter pilings, kingposts, and tieback elements. The design pattern from the email vault (Austin's correspondence with contractors who have asked about seawall use of EcoPile):

### The accepted seawall configuration

- **Batter pilings** — EcoPile poles driven at an angle, dying back into dead-men anchors set in the ground behind the wall. This is standard seawall practice and EcoPile handles it well.
- **Whaler boards** — running horizontally across the wall span behind the sheet panels, distributing the bolt load between batter piles and panels. **Strongly preferred** over individual point-load washers, both for structural performance and for aesthetic finish.
- **Curved washers / oversized washers** — required at any bolt connection where the bolt may land on a "moon" (concave hollow region between two truss legs) of the EcoPile cross-section, rather than on a triangle apex. Spreads the load and prevents point-loading the wall material over time.
- **Concrete cap with pile cutouts + rebar fill** — Austin's "advanced configuration." Pour a concrete cap across the top of the seawall with cutouts shaped to receive the EcoPile / KingPile tops; embed rebar from inside the piles up into the cap as it pours. The result is a monolithic structural unit that ties the entire wall together. Austin's verbatim phrase: *"damn near invincible wall."*

### Why this approach works

- The webbing and grooved bottom of the EcoPile resist settling under earth pressure better than smooth wood pilings.
- The full-system stiffness comes from the combination of vertical members + whaler distribution + (optional) concrete cap. No single component carries the full load.
- The PVC-fiberglass composition resists the soil/water chemistry that destroys steel sheet piling and is immune to the marine borers that destroy wood.

## Common customer questions

**Q: Can I use EcoPile for a seawall today, before the dedicated seawall product line launches?**
A: Yes. Batter pilings + whaler boards + curved washers is the proven configuration. Austin has multiple projects deployed with this approach. See the case study reference below.

**Q: How does EcoWall sheet piling compare to standard vinyl in installation?**
A: Same 0.25" profile, same panel handling, same shipping logistics — meaning the contractor's existing vinyl-sheet install workflow translates directly. The structural performance lift is "free" from a labor standpoint.

**Q: What's the difference between EcoPile and KingPile?**
A: KingPile is a heavier-duty variant of the EcoPile family, engineered for the higher structural loading typical of seawall kingposts (the vertical structural members that bracket large panels of sheet piling). The general EcoPile platform handles batter-pile and tieback duty in seawall service; KingPile is for the heavy-load kingpost positions. Spec sheet harmonization is in progress — confirm size ranges with the office before quoting.

## Common objections

**"How can I trust a product that hasn't fully shipped yet?"**
A: The components are proven separately. EcoPile has been in marine service for 8+ years across Florida, the Gulf Coast, North Carolina, and (recently) Michigan. The fiber-reinforced regrind material that goes into EcoWall is the same composite chemistry — Shoreline's been making it for nearly a decade. The seawall product *line* is being formalized and channel-positioned; the underlying material technology is field-proven.

**"What about steel or concrete sheet piling?"**
A: Steel rusts in saltwater service — corrosion is a question of *when*, not *if*. Concrete sheet piling is heavy, requires specialty equipment to install, and shears under cyclic storm loading (see the Hurricane Sally story in `ecopiile/talking-points.md` — same physics applies to seawalls). EcoWall is corrosion-immune, marine-organism-immune, lighter to handle, and uses standard sheet-piling install equipment.
