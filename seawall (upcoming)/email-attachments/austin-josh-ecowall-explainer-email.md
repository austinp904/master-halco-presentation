## Email body (verbatim)

Hey Josh!

Thanks again for your interest in the new vinyl sheet were offering. Ours is a bit unique, as the material it's made out of is significantly more rigid than the PVC that makes up other vinyl sheets. Ours is derived from our EcoPile compound, using chopped fiberglass fused into the PVC for a bunch stiffer product. You can see a comparison from standard "rigid" PVC to our compound in the attached video **"compound stiffness test"**. The blue pipe is our standard rigid material used for mining pipe, and the grey pipe is our first ever successful run of the fiber compound where we got the fibers to bond properly. Since this video was taken 7 years ago, we've more than doubled the number of fibers and rigidity in the formulation.

The main thing that drives the strength rating on these is the allowable moment, which is mostly derived from the materials' "modulus of elasticity". Just about every vinyl sheet pile manufacturer uses **380,000 psi** for their value, as that's the default for PVC of this grade, however with our material we can achieve up to **800,000 psi**. We are currently derating the strength as the product is newer, and we want to ensure we don't push too far, so our values on these data sheets (attached) currently reflect a safer margin with an MoE of only **550,000**. Our values still have the **industry standard 3x safety factor** applied.

This new panel is really the first true "composite" sheet pile. Most people use the term incorrectly with composite, talking about the pure FRP panels that are both wildly expensive and hard to work with. These are just pultruded fiberglass, and not a true "composite" in the sense that is a hybrid of different materials. Our material is extruded, just like other vinyl sheets, which is much more cost-effective and scalable. The technology we developed to put the chopped glass into the PVC and have the two bond together properly is our secret sauce, creating the first real composite in the sense that two different materials come together to enhance the characteristics of both. Because people mix the terminology up so much, I've been calling it a **"hybrid"**, not a composite.

This increase in flexural strength causes the allowable moment to dramatically [increase]. This first table shows a comparison of our Allowable moment to two competitor's sheets of the similar size. (all .25 inches thick, but slight variations in depth and width that could skew the numbers a bit). Roughly the same size means that it's roughly the same price, since at the end of the day, most of these plastics manufactures for the walls are selling finished product by the pound.

> EcoWall 24.250: **5,979 ft-lb/ft** allowable moment
> SG-325 (.25" thick): 2,960 ft-lb/ft → EcoWall is **+102.0%**
> ESP 3.1 (.25" thick): 3,129 ft-lb/ft → EcoWall is **+91.1%**

So the 24'' ecowall panel is about twice as strong as any other .25'' thick panels on the market. To get closer to the strength of our panel, you have to upsize to sheets that are .375–.4 inches thick, as seen here:

> **[CHART 2 — thicker-competitor comparison: `chart-2-allowable-moment-vs-thicker-competitors.png`]**
> EcoWall 24.250 (.25" thick): **5,979 ft-lb/ft**
> SG-625 (.385" thick): 6,507 ft-lb/ft → EcoWall is **−8.1%** (almost matched, at 65% the thickness)
> ESP 6.5 (.375" thick): 5,481 ft-lb/ft → EcoWall is **+9.1%** (beats it, at 67% the thickness)

So with our panel being priced similarly per pound, you can either offer a much stronger wall at the same price, or use more cost effective panels to hit the target allowable moments!

Currently we only have the smaller **.225'' x 18'' profile**, and the **.25'' x 24'' wide profile** available. We are waiting [to] get more feedback before pulling the trigger on the larger sizes, but based on the calculations/simulations, we should be able to get up to the strength of the beefy **.5 inch thick panels at just .3–.35 inches thick**, and we're talking about making a panel to compete with the monsters like **CMI's SG-950**, which is about ¾ inch thick, as with our material we should be able to achieve the same strength or greater at under **.5 inches**.

## Slide-builder cheat sheet

Three concepts here that are **not** in the brochures or pitch deck and **should** make it into the EcoWall slides:

1. **The modulus-of-elasticity story.** Industry default 380,000 psi → EcoWall achieves up to 800,000 psi → currently published at 550,000 psi (deliberately conservative) → with 3× industry-standard safety factor still applied. This is the engineering reason EcoWall outperforms — and the conservative public-spec number means the real-world performance margin is even higher than the comparison charts show.
2. **"Hybrid" vs. "composite" framing.** Most plastic-sheet competitors are pure-PVC. Most fiberglass competitors are pultruded fiberglass that get incorrectly called "composite." EcoWall is the first **true** hybrid — two materials chemically bonded to enhance both — and it's extruded (not pultruded), which is why it can be priced like a vinyl sheet rather than like a fiberglass sheet.
3. **Product roadmap.** Currently shipping: .225" × 18" and .25" × 24". Next: .3–.35" panel competing with .5" market profiles. Later: a sub-.5" panel competing with CMI's SG-950 (¾"). **This is the right "where we're going" story for a vendor-training slide** — gives Master Halco reps a sense that their customers' future spec needs will be coverable.
