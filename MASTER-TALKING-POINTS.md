## Why Shoreline, Why Now

Shoreline Plastics has been in business for 18 years, starting with vinyl sheet piling as the company's first product. As composite decking became the residential norm and the EPA's regulatory pressure on chromated copper arsenate (CCA) treated wood mounted, Shoreline pivoted into composite marine construction technologies — purpose-built to *replace* CCA-treated timber in the substrate (the part below the deck), where the toxicity problem was always worst.

The mission is simple and worth quoting directly on a slide:

> *"Modernize marine construction through sustainable composites that outlast and outperform wood, while sticking to a price point that makes the products attainable for residential and lighter commercial projects."*

That price-point clause is the entire reason Shoreline matters in a Master Halco distribution channel. Solid fiberglass pilings (Pearson FRP and similar) work — but they're priced beyond the average dock job. Shoreline's composites land at roughly **half the cost of comparable 100% fiberglass**, putting them inside the budget envelope that 80%+ of Master Halco's contractor and property-owner customers actually buy at.

### The CCA / EPA hook (use this early in the deck — it's the strongest "why now")

- 2003: EPA banned CCA-treated wood for general consumer use due to human-health and environmental impact. A carve-out was permitted for marine and "non-contact" timbers — *because at the time no cost-effective alternative existed.* That is no longer true.
- An estimated **2.5 million gallons of CCA treatment enters Florida waterways every year** through marine timber alone. Visual: *"that's like dumping two tank trucks into a Florida waterway, every single day."*
- 2019 EPA follow-up study reinforced toxicity findings beyond the threshold at which the agency typically takes action. EPA reference: `https://www.regulations.gov/document?D=EPA-HQ-OPP-2015-0349-0008`
- Several states have already banned any treated lumber that contacts water; others are walking back allowable treatment levels — meaning new wood pilings are *less* protected than older ones, **shortening their lifespan dramatically.** Where homeowners used to expect 20–30+ years of life from a wood pile, modern wood pilings are increasingly failing in less than half that.
- This is why composite piling adoption is accelerating *now* — the regulatory baseline and the wood quality have crossed the line where wood is no longer the obvious cheap default.

## Product Platform — Four Products, Three in Today's Pitch

The Shoreline platform is **SnapJacket, DuroSleeve, EcoPile, and EcoWall.** Today's training focuses on three: SnapJacket, EcoPile, and the seawall product line (EcoWall + KingPile). DuroSleeve is the technical brand for the underlying SnapJacket extrusion (it appears on submittal documents alongside SnapJacket) — treat them as one product family from a sales standpoint.

| Product | One-line description | Customer fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SnapJacket** | Patent-pending clamshell PVC piling repair jacket — locks around a damaged pile and is filled with concrete to restore structural integrity without removing the deck. | Anyone with a wood-piling dock or boathouse showing rot, marine-borer damage, or storm wear. Repair-minded. Highest-frequency, lowest-ticket product. |
| **EcoPile** | Vinyl-fiberglass composite structural piling with hexagram internal truss. Drop-in replacement for wood pilings using standard contractor tools. | New construction and full replacements. Highest revenue per project. The flagship. |
| **EcoWall (seawall product line)** | High-rigidity fiber-reinforced sheet panels for seawalls — ~70% stiffer than traditional vinyl sheets at the same 0.25" profile and same market price. Often paired with EcoPile-derived KingPile structural members. | Seawall and shoreline-protection projects. Currently positioned as a "preview" within the Master Halco relationship — strategy still being finalized. |

### Cross-product themes (good slide material)

- **Composite-technology platform** — every product solves a single underlying problem: replace toxic, decaying wood with a recycled-content composite that lasts decades longer at a price residential and light-commercial projects can actually afford.
- **Drop-in for the contractor** — SnapJacket and EcoPile use *standard* tools (Sawzall, powerwasher, bucket and shovel for SnapJacket; standard pile-driving rigs for EcoPile). No specialty equipment, no specialty training. This is critical messaging for Master Halco — their contractor customers don't want to retool.
- **Recycled content + recyclability** — EcoPile reuses up to **10 lbs of recycled PVC per linear foot of piling**. EcoWall uses regrind from other product lines. Everything is 100% recyclable at end of life.
- **Made in USA — Jacksonville, FL** — 300 Alton Box Road West, Jacksonville, FL 32218. Supports tariff-resilience and lead-time stories.
- **Warranties unique in the category** — EcoPile is the only piling on the market with a written warranty (currently 50 years per the most recent pitch deck). SnapJacket carries a 25-year warranty per the brochure. Note: the older EcoPile brochure in `ecopiile/` quotes 25-year — current truth per pitch deck is 50; harmonize on 50-year in any new collateral.
- **Hurricane-proven** — see the Pensacola / Hurricane Sally story in `ecopiile/talking-points.md`. This is the single most powerful proof point in the entire deck.
- **Multiple national building awards won on projects using EcoPile.**
