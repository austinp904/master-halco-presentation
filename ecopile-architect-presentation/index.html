The Old Default

Treated timber is familiar. It is also the weak link under premium waterfront work.

Owners upgrade decking, hardware, lifts, and finishes, then often leave the whole project sitting on a material exposed to rot, borers, checking, impact, and preservative concerns.

Failure Cycle

Replacing failed piles can mean dismantling the deck, lift, framing, and utilities above them.

Exposure

Marine piles live in wet, abrasive, UV-exposed conditions with constant biological and handling risk.

Better Baseline

Material Decision

The comparison is not pole price. It is installed lifecycle risk.

Treated Wood

  • Known material and familiar workflow.
  • Vulnerable to rot, decay, marine borers, checking, and replacement cycles.
  • Protective wraps add cost and slow deterioration, but do not make wood permanent.
  • Foundation replacement can mean tearing out the whole dock.

EcoPile

  • PVC/fiberglass composite designed for marine use.
  • Free from leaching wood preservatives, recyclable, and resistant to wood-boring organisms.
  • Best compared as whole-dock value: fewer replacement cycles beneath premium decking and hardware.
  • Submittal packet includes product data, SDS, engineering pages, buckling tables, and connection guidance.

Installation Reality

Standard pile-driving workflow, tighter material rules.

The packet names the equipment, handling, cutting, drilling, and connection practices that keep EcoPile in a familiar marine-construction workflow.

Driving

Drop, diesel, and vibratory drivers are covered. Use cushion blocks with larger equipment.

Handling

Use gripping straps or protected chains. The smooth outer shell can be slippery when wet.

Cutting & Drilling

Use a reciprocating saw with 8-12 TPI blade and auger or carbide-tipped drill bits.

Finish Work

Inspect after hauling, barge handling, and driving. Plan touch-up or cleaning before closeout.

Connection Detailing

Use bolted load spread, not lag-bolt bite.

EcoPile uses a semi-hollow composite profile with internal webbing. Structural attachments use through-bolts or all-thread, nuts and washers on both sides, plus curved washers or plates where loads concentrate.

Location, water exposure, current, ice/freeze concern, and access.
Geometry
Pile diameter, length, exposed height above mudline, and spacing.
Loads
Deck, roof, lift, lateral, uplift, vessel, and freestanding conditions.
Soils
Geotech report, embedment target, jetting/pilot-hole plan, and blow counts.
Connections
Through-bolts, washer sizes, notching, brackets, caps, and load spread.
Equipment
Driving method, hammer type, vibratory driver access, and handling setup.
Common Questions

Fast answers to the objections that come up first.

Does it flex?

Yes. Composite piling can deflect differently than timber. Check exposed height, lateral load, bracing, and serviceability with the project engineer.

What skin friction value?

Do not use one blanket value. The internal webbing and soil compaction are part of the performance case, but final assumptions belong with the geotech and EOR.

Do we fill it?

Not as the default. Consider concrete fill when the design needs extra stiffness, side-load resistance, ballast, repair strategy, or capacity.

Will it look perfect after driving?

It is a premium manufactured finish, but long poles are hauled, handled, barged, and driven. Touch-up and cleaning guidance belongs in the closeout plan.

Specifier Language

Baseline spec language for design review and submittal routing.

Composite Marine Piling: Provide Shoreline Plastics EcoPile PVC/fiberglass composite marine piling, or approved equal, in diameters and lengths indicated on the drawings. Submittals: Submit product data, SDS, engineering report, buckling/load information, installation guidance, and bolted connection guidance for review. Installation: Install in accordance with manufacturer guidance, project drawings, geotechnical recommendations, and requirements of the engineer of record. Engineering: Project-specific pile size, embedment, exposed height, load capacity, connection design, and code compliance shall be verified by the engineer of record and geotechnical engineer.

The final specification still needs project-specific pile sizing, embedment, exposed-height checks, connection design, code review, and stamped engineering responsibility.
